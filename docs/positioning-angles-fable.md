# Apptek positioning angles — the whole shelf (Fable)

**Status:** Strategic brief for Bera — pick 2–3 angles to develop next. Not site copy.
**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Builds on:** v3 landing copy (`docs/copy-v3-fable.md`), First Mate's seven seed outcomes
**Rules honored:** "digital helpers," approve-before-send, no AI-scare headlines, real busywork only
**Read time:** ~5 minutes if you stick to the tables and the bold lines. Deep-dive blocks are there when you want them.

---

## TL;DR — my pick

Busywork is the **brand frame**, not the **sale**. "Get your busywork handled" tells an owner what kind of company we are. It doesn't tell them which bleeding to stop first. The pilot should be sold as a **leak** — a named place where money or goodwill quietly drains — and busywork stays as the umbrella every leak sits under.

If I had to pick three to develop now:

1. **Don't lose the lead** (split into *answer fast* + *win the quote*) — the most measurable revenue leak an SMB has, and the easiest to demo live.
2. **Get paid** — zero AI-theater risk, universal pain, and the helper plays "bad cop" so the owner keeps the relationship.
3. **Bring them back** (repeat / recall / referral — missing from the seed list) — the cheapest revenue in the whole document; it's just remembering on time.

Marketing ("Get found & chosen") is real and Bera is right to want it — but it's **four different products** with wildly different theater risk. Sell the boring one (*know where customers come from*) inside the pilot, and keep content/ads for the expand phase after the owner trusts the helpers' voice.

## 1. Outcome group: **Time back**

### 1. Reclaim the week *(First Mate #1 — the current site)*
- **Promise:** The grind of the week arrives drafted; you approve and get your attention back.
- **Helper jobs:** Overnight inquiry drafts · follow-up nudges · scheduling moves · reminders · invoice nudges · routine updates.
- **Who hurts:** Everyone in the 3–50 band. That's the problem — it's true of everyone and urgent for no one.
- **Theater risk:** Low. Nobody thinks a drafted reply is a magic trick. The risk here is **vagueness**, not hype: "save time" is what every tool says.
- **Fit:** **Umbrella.** Keep it as the hero frame and the company's personality. Don't sell the pilot on it — sell the pilot on one of the leaks below and let "reclaim the week" be what the owner *feels* afterward.
- **Opinion:** This is the best *brand* line Apptek has and the weakest *sales* line. Both can be true.

### 2. The Monday note
- **Promise:** Know where the week stands before it starts — in one plain-English note, not a dashboard.
- **Helper jobs:** Monday 7am note: leads waiting on a reply · quotes open by value · invoices overdue · reviews needing a word · calendar gaps and double-bookings · anything that went quiet last week.
- **Who hurts:** Owners at 10–50 who've lost line-of-sight. The 3-person shop already knows all this in their head.
- **Theater risk:** **Med.** "Insights dashboards" are where AI theater goes to live. Keep it a text note in the owner's inbox with numbers they can check. No charts until asked.
- **Fit:** **Expand 1.** It's the natural byproduct once three or four helpers are running — the playbook, made daily. Free-ish to add, high perceived value, and it's the thing that makes the owner *notice* the helpers working.

## 2. Outcome group: **More revenue in**

### 3. Answer fast *(First Mate #2, first half)*
- **Promise:** Every new inquiry gets a real, in-your-voice answer within the hour — with your OK.
- **Helper jobs:** Web form / email / voicemail → drafted reply · missed-call text-back draft ("Got it, Bera will call by 3") · quote-request acknowledgement with next step · one lead log, one place · "still interested?" nudge at day 3 / 7 / 14.
- **Who hurts:** **Trades** most (HVAC, roofing, plumbing — the lead calls three companies and hires whoever answers). Clinics with new-patient inquiries. Agencies with inbound they're too busy to work.
- **Theater risk:** **Low.** The pain is visible (the owner *knows* they replied Thursday to a Monday lead) and the proof is a clock.
- **Fit:** **Pilot — the best one.** Most measurable, most demoable, and the lead log it creates is the raw material for #4, #5, and #6a.
- **Tension to name:** speed vs approve-before-send. A draft that waits until 9pm isn't fast. The pilot answer: acknowledgements ("we got it, here's when you'll hear back") are the classic **first thing an owner loosens**, and the FAQ already promises that path. Substantive replies still wait for the OK.

### 4. Win the quote *(First Mate #2, second half — worth splitting out)*
- **Promise:** Quotes you've already sent stop dying quietly.
- **Helper jobs:** Quote sent → follow-up drafts at day 2 / 7 / 14 with escalating warmth · "anything unclear in the estimate?" reply · expiring-quote reminder · open-quotes-by-value tally every Friday · lost-quote "what did you go with?" note (free market research).
- **Who hurts:** **Trades and agencies** — anyone with bespoke pricing and a proposal stage. Practices rarely quote.
- **Theater risk:** **Low.** It's a calendar and a tone.
- **Fit:** **Pilot.** For a trade doing $5k jobs, three revived quotes pays for a year of Apptek. Often the single highest-dollar leak on the list.
- **Opinion:** Speed-to-lead and quote follow-through get lumped together and shouldn't be — different moment, different tone, different owner (the estimator vs the front desk). Separate them in the pitch; they share one helper under the hood.

### 5. Bring them back *(not on the seed list — the biggest gap)*
- **Promise:** Past customers come back on time and bring a friend.
- **Helper jobs:** 90-day "how's it holding up?" check-in · annual service / recall reminder ("furnace tune-up month," "six-month cleaning") · referral ask drafted right after a 5-star review or a great outcome · dormant-customer list (nothing in 12 months) with a warm wake-up note · seasonal "book before the rush" nudge.
- **Who hurts:** **Clinics** (recall is literally the revenue model), **trades** (maintenance contracts), **agencies** (past clients are the warmest leads they ignore), salons, tutors, vets.
- **Theater risk:** **Low.** This is remembering, on time, politely. No one calls a recall postcard "AI."
- **Fit:** **Pilot.** Cheapest revenue in the document — the customer already trusts you and you already have their email.
- **Opinion:** This straddles "marketing" and "keep customers." I put it under revenue because the owner counts it in bookings, and because it's the honest answer to "how do I grow?" for most SMBs: not a new channel, but a full calendar from people who already know you.

### 6. Get found & chosen *(First Mate #3 — four products wearing one coat)*

Bera's explicit interest, and the right instinct: owners *want* to be told how to grow. But "marketing" splits into four angles with very different risk. Treat them separately.

#### 6a. Know where customers come from
- **Promise:** Know which channel actually brings the customers you keep — not the ones you're paying for.
- **Helper jobs:** "How did you hear about us?" asked and logged on every inquiry · monthly leads-by-source tally · cost per lead per channel from ad receipts + the lead log · flag when a channel dries up or a source spikes · which source produces customers who *pay and return* (join with #5 and #10).
- **Who hurts:** Anyone spending $500–5k/month on Google LSA, Meta, Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack without a straight answer on which one works. That's most trades and many clinics.
- **Theater risk:** **Low** if it's plain arithmetic in a plain note. **High** the moment it's called "AI marketing insights."
- **Fit:** **Pilot component.** It's a byproduct of #3's lead log. Sell it as "we'll tell you where your customers actually came from" and the owner leans in.

#### 6b. A steady drumbeat
- **Promise:** Posts, updates, and emails go out on a steady beat — drafted from what actually happened this week, in your voice, with your OK.
- **Helper jobs:** Monthly newsletter draft from the month's real jobs · Google Business Profile post draft from a job photo + two lines · before/after write-up · seasonal reminder email ("it's gutter season") · "we're hiring / new service / holiday hours" notices · reply drafts to comments and DMs.
- **Who hurts:** Owners who *know* they should post and haven't since March.
- **Theater risk:** **Med–High.** "AI content" is the most-recognized AI theater on earth, and customers can smell it. The **only** defensible version: every draft starts from a real input (a job done, a question asked, a review received). No topic generators, no "10 tips" listicles, no stock imagery.
- **Fit:** **Expand 2.** Sell it after the owner has approved 200 helper drafts and trusts the voice. Selling it first is how Apptek becomes "the AI content guys."

#### 6c. Ad spend watch
- **Promise:** Nobody wastes your ad money quietly — you see spend vs leads every week and decide.
- **Helper jobs:** Weekly spend vs leads vs booked jobs note · alert draft when cost-per-lead jumps · "pause this / shift that" recommendation for owner approval · monthly snapshot of competitor ads and offers · plain-English translation of the agency's report.
- **Who hurts:** Owners running ads through a platform or a small agency they don't fully trust.
- **Theater risk:** **Med.** "AI optimizes your ads" is a crowded, hype-heavy shelf, and Google/Meta already claim it. Apptek's honest role is **the owner's eyes on the platform or the agency** — not another optimizer.
- **Fit:** **Expand 2**, and only after 6a exists (you can't watch spend against leads you haven't counted). Recommendations, never hands on the account, unless Bera decides otherwise (see open questions).

#### 6d. Local market read
- **Promise:** Know what people around you are asking for and what your competitors are charging.
- **Helper jobs:** Monthly digest of competitor pricing and offers · themes in competitors' reviews (what customers wish they did) · questions people ask in local groups · seasonal demand signals · a one-page "should we add this service?" read.
- **Who hurts:** Owners planning a new service, a second location, or a price change.
- **Theater risk:** **High.** Research is easy to make look impressive and hard to make actionable. It's the angle most likely to produce a beautiful PDF nobody uses.
- **Fit:** **One-off add-on**, priced as a project, bundled into a quarterly review — never a standalone helper or a headline. Don't lead with it.

## 3. Outcome group: **Keep the customers you have**

### 7. Keep customers happy *(First Mate #4, support half)*
- **Promise:** Every customer question gets a fast, correct, human-approved answer — no bot, ever.
- **Helper jobs:** FAQ-style reply drafts built from *your* past answers (hours, pricing, parking, prep, warranty) · appointment / order status replies · post-job "how did it go?" note · complaint triage — flagged, drafted, **never** auto-sent · a growing "how we answer things" page the owner owns.
- **Who hurts:** Clinic front desks drowning in portal messages, agency account managers, any local operator with a busy inbox.
- **Theater risk:** **Med–High.** "AI customer support" is the most *hated* AI in consumer life. The whole differentiation is: **the customer never talks to a helper.** The helper drafts; the human sends. Say that early and often.
- **Fit:** **Pilot** when inbox volume is the visible pain (front desk says "I spend two hours a day answering the same six questions"). Otherwise **Expand 1** — it reuses everything #3 built.

### 8. Reputation watch *(First Mate #7)*
- **Promise:** You hear about every review and mention the day it lands, with a reply ready.
- **Helper jobs:** Daily digest of new Google / Yelp / Facebook reviews · thank-you reply drafts · recovery reply drafts for the 2-stars (flagged, owner decides) · review-request drafts after a good outcome (Google-safe; Yelp-aware) · pattern flag ("three mentions of 'wait time' this month").
- **Who hurts:** Clinics, restaurants, trades, salons — anyone whose next customer reads reviews first.
- **Theater risk:** **Low–Med.** The risk is the sea of "reputation management" vendors, not AI hype. Differentiate on *owner-approved, owner-voiced* replies.
- **Fit:** **Pilot-sized add-on.** Small, self-contained, and the best candidate for the **first loosening** — thank-you replies to 5-stars are about as low-risk as a send gets. Keep it separate from #7; it's a tiny product the owner can say yes to in one breath.
- **Watch:** Clinics can't mention treatments or confirm someone is a patient in a public reply (HIPAA). Reply drafts need a clinic mode.

### 9. Notice who's gone quiet
- **Promise:** Notice when a good customer goes quiet — before they're gone.
- **Helper jobs:** Watch for missed recurring appointments · retainer clients with no contact in 30 days · a late payment as an early churn signal · draft a warm, specific check-in (not "just checking in") · monthly "at-risk" list for the owner.
- **Who hurts:** **Agencies** with retainers, clinics, gyms and studios, subscription-ish services.
- **Theater risk:** **Med.** "Churn prediction" is AI theater unless it's plain rules the owner can read ("hasn't booked in 60 days"). Keep the rules visible in the playbook.
- **Fit:** **Expand 1.** Needs a couple of months of history to mean anything.

## 4. Outcome group: **Cash in**

### 10. Get paid *(First Mate #5)*
- **Promise:** Invoices go out on time and get chased politely — without you being the bad guy.
- **Helper jobs:** Invoice draft the moment a job is marked done · reminder cadence (3 days before / due / +7 / +14 / +30) with escalating firmness the owner sets · deposit request drafted at booking · "payment received, thank you" note · Friday aging summary · payment-plan offer draft for the big overdue ones.
- **Who hurts:** **Trades** (chasing homeowners), **agencies** (net-30 that becomes net-75), practices with patient balances. Universal.
- **Theater risk:** **Low.** No one has ever called a payment reminder an AI demo.
- **Fit:** **Pilot.** Universal pain, immediate proof (days-to-paid), and a real emotional benefit: the helper takes the awkward job, the owner keeps the relationship.
- **Watch:** First-party reminders are fine; anything that reads like a threat or a third-party collector is not. Tone caps belong in the playbook.

### 11. Stop the leaks
- **Promise:** Know what you're paying for every month and stop paying for what you don't use.
- **Helper jobs:** Monthly recurring-charge list from statements · flag price increases · renewal-date reminders 30 days out · cancel / renegotiate email drafts.
- **Who hurts:** 10–50 person agencies with SaaS sprawl; anyone who's found a $79/month charge from 2023.
- **Theater risk:** **Low.**
- **Fit:** **Later.** A nice second-quarter "found money" win. Slightly outside the core; keep it minor.

## 5. Outcome group: **Run a tight week**

### 12. Show up prepared *(First Mate #6)*
- **Promise:** Every appointment confirmed, every visit briefed, no-shows down.
- **Helper jobs:** Confirmation drafts at T-48h and T-24h · reschedule handling with two offered slots · day-before job sheet (address, notes, materials, what happened last visit) · waitlist-fill draft when someone cancels · weekly staffing-gap note ("Thursday has 9 visits and 2 techs").
- **Who hurts:** **Clinics** (every no-show is an empty chair you paid for), trades with routed crews, salons, tutors, anyone appointment-based. Weakest for agencies.
- **Theater risk:** **Low.**
- **Fit:** **Pilot** for appointment businesses; not for the rest. Proof is a no-show rate before and after, which owners already track in their gut.

### 13. Applicants hear back
- **Promise:** Applicants hear back the same day and interviews get booked without the back-and-forth.
- **Helper jobs:** Applicant acknowledgement · screening-question follow-up · interview scheduling · onboarding checklist reminders · shift-swap coordination drafts.
- **Who hurts:** Trades and clinics with turnover; seasonal hirers.
- **Theater risk:** **Med.** "AI hiring" is legally loaded (EEOC guidance, NYC Local Law 144). The safe line: **correspondence and scheduling only — never ranking, screening out, or deciding.**
- **Fit:** **Later.** Seasonal and legally fenced. Only when an owner asks.

### 14. Paperwork never sneaks up
- **Promise:** Renewals, licenses, and required documents never surprise you.
- **Helper jobs:** License / insurance renewal reminders · certificate-of-insurance requests to subs · vendor W-9 chasing · recurring form drafts · credentialing deadline nudges.
- **Who hurts:** Trades (licenses, COIs), clinics (credentialing), agencies (contract renewals).
- **Theater risk:** **Low.**
- **Fit:** **Later** — a small add-on the owner discovers they want after the first missed renewal.

### 15. The team knows without a meeting
- **Promise:** The team knows what's happening this week without another stand-up.
- **Helper jobs:** Friday wrap draft · job-status updates to staff · handoff notes between shifts · "what changed this week" note for the manager.
- **Who hurts:** The 30–50 end of the band, where a manager layer has appeared and nobody's sure who knows what.
- **Theater risk:** **Low–Med.** Internal, low stakes.
- **Fit:** **Expand 1** for the larger pilots only. Skip for the 3-person shop.

## Wedge → expand: what to sell first, what to package later

**Principle:** Every expansion should be either a **loosening** (a job that earned trust runs with a lighter touch) or a **neighbor** (a new helper that reuses inputs an existing helper already has). Never sell a helper that needs data we don't have yet.

### Phase 0 — the wedge (Founding Pilots)
Sell **one leak, two or three helpers**, proof in two weeks. Pick the leak by vertical:

| Vertical | Lead with | Plus | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Trades** | #3 Answer fast + #4 Win the quote | #10 Get paid | Lead → quote → cash is their whole week; one lead log powers all three. |
| **Clinics / practices** | #12 Show up prepared | #5 Bring them back (recall), #8 Reputation | Empty chairs and recall *are* the revenue model. Keep PHI out of scope until there's a HIPAA story (see questions). |
| **Agencies** | #4 Win the quote (proposals) + #10 Get paid | #9 Notice who's gone quiet | Proposal follow-through and net-75 invoices are the agency owner's two private embarrassments. |
| **Local operators** (salon, studio, restaurant) | #12 Show up prepared + #8 Reputation | #5 Bring them back | Appointments and reviews decide their week. |

Always included, free, as a byproduct: **#6a Know where customers come from** — because the lead log exists anyway and it's the line that makes owners lean forward.

### Phase 1 — Expand 1 (month 2–3, same data, more trust)
- **#2 The Monday note** — the helpers' work, made visible.
- **#7 Keep customers happy** — the inbox helper widens from inquiries to all customer questions.
- **#5 Bring them back** — if it wasn't in the pilot.
- **#9 Notice who's gone quiet** — now there's history.
- **First loosenings:** inquiry acknowledgements, 5-star thank-yous, appointment confirmations. Owner chooses; owner can tighten back.

### Phase 2 — Expand 2 (quarter 2+, new trust: the owner's public voice)
- **#6b A steady drumbeat** — only from real inputs, only after the owner has approved a few hundred drafts.
- **#6c Ad spend watch** — only after 6a has three months of leads-by-source.
- **#6d Local market read** — as a quarterly one-off, never a helper.

### Phase 3 — Later / on request
- #11 Stop the leaks · #13 Applicants hear back · #14 Paperwork · #15 Team wrap.

### What this does to the offer
"Reclaim the week" stays the hero. Underneath it, the **pilot menu** becomes four named leaks (answer fast, win the quote, get paid, show up prepared) with "where customers come from" thrown in. That's a menu an owner can point at in ten seconds, which the current "two or three busywork jobs" can't quite do. *(Not a copy change for this PR — just the direction.)*

## Angles to avoid or de-emphasize (and why)

| Avoid / de-emphasize | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **"AI transformation" / "AI strategy for your business"** | No job, no proof, no owner asks for it. Pure theater. Violates "real busywork only." |
| **A chatbot on the customer's website** | The most recognized and most disliked AI in consumer life; breaks approve-before-send by design. Missed-call text-back drafts, yes; a bot talking to customers, no. |
| **A voice-AI receptionist** | Tempting for trades, but it's the same objection out loud. Not yet; maybe never under this brand. |
| **"AI runs your ads" / autonomous ad optimization** | Google and Meta already claim this; agencies claim it louder; attribution fights are endless. Be the owner's eyes, not another optimizer. |
| **Content mills ("50 posts a month," SEO blog factories)** | Google's scaled-content policies, brand damage, and it's the fastest way to be labeled "the AI content guys." Drumbeat from real inputs only. |
| **Cold outbound at scale** | Spam, deliverability, reputation — and the opposite of the warm "helpers" voice. Warm follow-ups to people who asked, yes. Blasting strangers, no. |
| **Hiring decisions (screening, ranking, rejecting)** | Legal exposure (EEOC, NYC LL144). Correspondence and scheduling only. |
| **Bookkeeping, tax, payroll** | CPA territory and liability. Nudges and reminders, yes; the books, no. |
| **Clinical, legal, or financial *advice* drafting** | Liability and licensing. For clinics, scheduling/admin only; keep PHI out of drafts until there's a real HIPAA/BAA posture. |
| **Dashboards and "insights" as a product** | Theater with charts. The Monday note in plain text does the job and is harder to fake. |
| **Replacing the owner's tools / a new platform** | The site promises no migration. Stay a quiet layer on top. |
| **Generic "productivity" or "efficiency" language** | Says nothing, competes with everyone. Name the leak. |
| **"Reclaim the week" as the only pitch** | It's the frame, not the sale (see TL;DR). |

## If Apptek were a shelf of offers — naming options

The v3 hero card already names helpers by **job** (Inbox helper, Follow-up helper, Scheduling helper). Keep that for the *individual* helpers. The question is how to name the **shelves** — the groups an owner picks from. Five options, plain English, in rough order of my preference:

1. **Verb shelves — "Answer. Follow up. Get paid. Stay known."**
   Four verbs, four shelves; each helper lives under one. Reads like a to-do list the owner recognizes. *Pro:* fast to scan, easy to point at. *Con:* "stay known" is the weakest of the four; alternates: "stay remembered," "keep in touch."

2. **Leak shelves — "The Lead Leak · The Quote Leak · The Cash Leak · The Calendar Leak"**
   Names the bleeding, not the bandage. *Pro:* the most sales-sharp option; owners self-diagnose in one glance. *Con:* slightly negative for a warm brand; works better in the pilot menu than in the hero.

3. **Front-of-house / back-office — "Front-desk helpers · Follow-through helpers · Back-office helpers · Word-of-mouth helpers"**
   Groups by *where in the business* the helper sits. *Pro:* instantly familiar to service owners; "follow-through" is a nice ownable word. *Con:* four categories is one more than people remember; "word-of-mouth helpers" is charming but may undersell 6a–6c.

4. **"Never" shelves — "Never miss a lead · Never chase an invoice · Never lose a customer quietly · Never walk in cold"**
   Outcome as a vow. *Pro:* emotional, memorable. *Con:* "never" over-promises; approve-before-send means we *reduce*, not *never*. Use in conversation, not print.

5. **Owner's-week shelves — "The morning helpers · The follow-up helpers · The Friday helpers"**
   Groups by *when* in the week the work lands (overnight inquiries → daytime follow-ups → Friday invoices and wrap). *Pro:* uniquely on-brand with "the week"; nobody else will name it this way. *Con:* less obvious what's inside each; needs a subtitle.

**Recommendation:** Verb shelves (1) for the public menu; leak language (2) inside the pilot conversation ("which of these four is bleeding?"); keep job-named helpers underneath. Skip "never." Nothing here uses front door, specialists, runbook, ops automation, or bot ops.

## Open questions for Bera

**Wedge**
1. Which vertical are the first three Founding Pilots most likely to come from? The wedge changes materially between trades (leads/quotes/cash) and clinics (calendar/recall/reviews).
2. Are we willing to be **measured on a leak metric** (time-to-reply, days-to-paid, no-show rate) instead of "drafts delivered"? Yes makes the pilot far more sellable and turns every pilot into a case study; it also means we own the number.

**Approval line**
3. Where's the line for time-sensitive jobs on day one? Specifically: can an inquiry acknowledgement ("we got it, you'll hear from Bera by 3pm") go out without a per-message OK in a pilot, or does that wait for the owner to loosen it after week two?
4. Do we publish a **"what helpers will never do"** list (no bots talking to customers, no hiring decisions, no books, no medical/legal content)? I think it's a trust asset; it also fences the avoid list above.

**Marketing**
5. Does Apptek ever have **hands on the ad account** (Google/Meta), or strictly read reports and recommend? Access, liability, and pricing all change.
6. For "a steady drumbeat," are we comfortable drafting anything that appears *publicly* under the owner's name, or only one-to-one messages? That decides whether 6b is Expand 2 or Avoid.

**Access and compliance**
7. Which tools do the likely pilots actually run on — QuickBooks, Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Jane, Calendly, HubSpot, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365? "Get paid" and "show up prepared" live or die on those connections.
